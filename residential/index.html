No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Nobody likes a surprise on the invoice. So St. George HVAC quotes every St. George job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the equipment's age, fuel type, and tonnage, refrigerant levels, coil and heat exchanger condition, and the ductwork feeding each room. You'll know what's wrong, what the fix involves, and what it costs before you say yes.

The trouble with HVAC failures is that many of them develop where you can't see them. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down a little at a time, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, have the system checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Do you know how old your system is? When a furnace or AC gets past the 12-year mark, it's worth thinking about the next one before the current one forces the issue. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when choices are limited and everyone is uncomfortable. Our team knows how the St. George climate wears on equipment and will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Seasonal Tune-Ups

Maintenance Plans for St. George Homes

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the idea behind our seasonal plans. On each scheduled visit, we clean coils and burners, check the refrigerant charge, test the electrical components, inspect the heat exchanger, flush the condensate drain, and swap in a new filter, all matched to the equipment in your home.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. An older furnace gets a closer look than a brand-new heat pump, and a dusty area means condenser coils foul faster. We set up spring cooling and fall heating visits around your equipment and climate, so every tune-up helps keep the system efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a consistent maintenance schedule cuts operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Proper Sizing

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. St. George HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation that accounts for square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Picking a new system comes down to more than a SEER2 number. Our technician will compare furnace-and-AC setups with heat pumps, and ducted systems with ductless mini-splits, laying out operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Families with a growing household often do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds tighter temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The HVAC Repairs St. George Homes Need Most

No cooling, a furnace that won't ignite, a frozen evaporator coil, a slow refrigerant leak: each of these problems has its own cause and calls for a different repair. St. George HVAC diagnoses the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that short-cycles, a thermostat that loses its settings, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On cooling systems, it's usually a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it's more often an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaking ductwork bleeding conditioned air into the attic gets sealed, and we recover and recharge the refrigerant once the leak itself is repaired. Some of these issues are just inconvenient, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a genuine safety or water-damage risk, so we treat it with urgency.
