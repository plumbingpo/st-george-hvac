Complete Heating & Cooling Services for Salt Lake City Properties

Heating and cooling around here covers a lot of ground: aging furnaces on the older streets, newer heat pumps in recent construction, ductless mini-splits in additions and converted garages, and packaged units on commercial buildings. Our team works on every one of those systems. Our technicians have years in the field, and we've learned how the weather here, the age of local homes, and code requirements affect the way a system performs and fails.

A typical week for our residential technicians might include capacitor and contactor replacements, flame sensor cleanings, refrigerant leak repairs, blower motor swaps, and at least one full system replacement or ductless addition. When we do replace equipment, we size it with a room-by-room load calculation rather than matching the old unit. A correctly sized system keeps rooms even, runs efficiently, and steers clear of the short-cycling that kills compressors early. Beyond repairs and replacements, we recommend seasonal tune-ups that clean coils, confirm the refrigerant charge, test safety controls, and measure static pressure, the best single step for longer equipment life.

Commercial service makes up a large share of our work as well. We service rooftop units, split systems, and ventilation equipment for restaurants, shops, offices, and multi-unit properties, knowing that every hour of downtime hurts your bottom line. To keep disruptions short, we carry common parts on every truck, diagnose fast, and plan larger jobs around your operating hours whenever possible.

The urgent calls we run usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. Since our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these are wrapped up on the first trip, at a price you approve before we start.

A few minutes of caution while you wait for us can head off a much bigger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us right away.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Salt Lake City

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that range and also shows higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
